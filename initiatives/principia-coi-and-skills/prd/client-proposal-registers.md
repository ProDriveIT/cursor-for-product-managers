**From:** Pro Drive

## 1. The requirements

Principia wants a single place, inside the Microsoft 365 the firm already uses, where:

1. A member of staff can record a conflict of interest. The example given is work for another company.
2. A member of staff can keep their skills up to date.
3. They change the entry they already made. A correction does not create a second copy.
4. All of one person's skills sit together, so a reader sees that person, not a list sorted by the time things were typed in.
5. Each person sees only their own skills and their own interests.
6. A small number of named people can see everyone's records.
7. Some fields are a dropdown. Some are free text.
8. You can produce a report later if you need one. A dashboard is not part of this proposal.
9. There is no new Microsoft subscription and no monthly fee per person.

About 50 people will use it.

## 2. The design

Everyone uses one form in Microsoft Teams. The records sit in Principia's own Microsoft 365. Pro Drive sets this up. Principia owns the records.

**One approach.** This is the approach that meets the requirements above and stays on the Microsoft 365 you already pay for. We are not asking you to choose between systems.

**Who sees what.**

| Who | What they see |
|-----|----------------|
| Each employee | Only their own skills and their own interests |
| Two or three people you name | Everyone's records, one person at a time, with a spreadsheet download when they need a pack |
| Anyone else | Nothing of a colleague's record |

The named people will see outside interests. Keep that group small.

If the person who allocates work needs to see skills, they can be given skills only. They see outside interests only if you also name them in the small group. You can decide that after sign-off.

**How a record is kept.**

- A skill is one entry per person. Saving again updates that entry.
- An interest is one entry. A correction updates it. A genuinely new interest is a new entry.
- When an interest ends, the person marks it ended. The entry stays. People cannot delete an interest.
- If someone has no current interest, they confirm that on one entry and update the date when they review it. If they already have a current interest, that confirmation is not offered. Reviewing the form does not wipe an interest that is already there.

**Optional extra, decided after the form is in use.** People who already have Microsoft Copilot can also update their own record in a private Teams chat. Colleagues still cannot see each other's records. We switch the chat on only after we check that their Copilot licence covers it. If it does not, the chat stays off and everyone uses the form. There is nothing extra to buy from Microsoft either way.

A dashboard is a later conversation, for a named person who needs one. It would need its own licence, so it sits outside this sign-off.

The Copilot chat, if you want it later, is one further working day.

## 3. What people do

**A member of staff** opens Teams and opens the form. They see their own skills, each with a level and a notes box. They change a level and save. The same skill is still one entry. They add a skill from a dropdown. Skills they already have are not on that dropdown.

They see their outside interests on the same form. They correct one, or they add a new one. To close an interest they mark it ended. The entry remains.

If they have no current interest, they confirm "nothing to declare" and the date updates on that one entry. If they already have a current interest, they review those entries instead. The form does not offer a button that would clear them.

They never see a colleague's page.

**A named person** opens the same records and picks a person. They see that person's skills together, and that person's interests. They can download a spreadsheet of the whole set when they need to send it on. They do not get a separate system.

**Someone who is not named** sees only their own form, the same as any other member of staff.
